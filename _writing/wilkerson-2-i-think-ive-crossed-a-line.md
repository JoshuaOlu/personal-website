Dear Dr. Wilkerson,

**Subject: I Think I’ve Crossed a Line and I Don’t Know What to Do**

Good morning Dr. Wilkerson.

It’s Fola again, from Lagos. It’s around 2:17am here in Lagos. The lights have been out for hours, the air is cool, and still **I can’t sleep**. Something has been riding bicycles in my head and I can’t seem to put it to rest.

Maybe writing to you will steady my mind a little. It’s been about six months and I didn’t get any response to my first email so I’m not sure you’ll respond to this either, but I’ll write anyway.

Today is the last day of accreditation for my department. Once the sun comes up, they’ll just be checking some files and then they’ll leave, I think. What I want to talk about really is what happened yesterday and why its the reason I’m still awake.

First, I must give you more context, Ma.

About 4 weeks ago, my department started preparing us for accreditation. Being an underfunded department in a public institution, we students had to help with almost everything that involved physical work. A lot of housekeeping, a lot of “new” equipment, and about five “new” lecturers, one of them being a very respected Professor in the field.

But after all this came the grooming of students for the accreditation. We were coached, drilled, and corrected, almost like we were rehearsing for a play... One major emphasis was on Labs and software. We were told to never acknowledge the use of pirated software in classes or labs.

We were told to deny any knowledge on the use of these and claim that all our work was done in the faculty computer labs. I think the department is just scared.

There has been a renewed interest for a few months now on students using licensed software for academic work by accreditation bodies. The accreditation bodies too have to stand by it due to global best practices. The question I ask myself now is that **why do I have to lie to protect a** **facade when we all quietly know that this decay exists in our system??**

After different sessions of training us students for the accreditation, rumors began to circulate about Kunle, a final year student, who had told the accreditation team, during one of the previous accreditation, that one of the staff presented by the department wasn’t actually a staff.

The **rumors say that he didn’t graduate till three years after that.** Unknown to him, one of the members of the accreditation team at that time was a close friend of the HOD and had informally told him about Kunle’s actions after the end of the accreditation exercise.

So, when they called me yesterday, I prayed, oh, I prayed. I prayed that they shouldn’t ask me any questions that would need me to lie. Honestly, it was difficult. I’m a Christian, lying for me is not just morally wrong, it’s a sin. So when they were asking me questions in the fully air-conditioned office of the HOD, I was sweating. I went in so tense.

At first, the questions were alright. They asked what it was like as a student, they asked about the quality of the learning experience, and other things that my department did really well to be honest. Just as they were about to let me go, the only woman amongst them wearing a red suit asked me the questions I dreaded.

“What about software?” she asked, “... have you used any in the course of your study?” to which I responded “I have”. Almost like that was not enough, she asked directly, “do you use pirated software or licensed software in your classes?”.

At that point, I really just wanted the ground to open up its mouth and swallow me, but without thinking twice, **I said “No”. It came out too easily.** My hands were twitching, and it felt like she questioned me for an eternity. She pressed further, but I kept to the script we were trained on.

Dr. Wilkerson, after I left school yesterday, I went to church and I honestly couldn’t pray, I tried to sing, I couldn’t. Even now, I still don’t think I can look myself in the mirror.

**What am I becoming?** Today, I lied about software to protect myself and my department but how far will I go? You said *“ethics is engineering”*, honestly, for me, this is not just unethical anymore. If I have to lie to protect myself, it’s immoral.

So, I still can’t sleep. And maybe I’m exaggerating the whole thing; maybe it’s just exhaustion and the weight of staring at a screen in the dark. But I’m not sure this is the kind of person I want to become.

Warm regards,  
**Fola Adeoluwa**  
Mechatronics Engineering Undergraduate Student  
Microris University of Technology (MUT), Lagos
