Dear Dr. Wilkerson,

**Subject: “Following Up from Lagos - A Question About Software”**

I hope this email finds you well. My name is Fola Adeoluwa. I am a Mechatronics Engineering student at Microris University of Technology (MUT), Lagos, and I attended your keynote address at the West African Engineering Education Summit here in Lagos about six months ago. I was honestly really inspired by your speech and even though I can’t pretend to understand every single thing you spoke about, a few things stood out to me.

Before your speech, I never really questioned some things. They just seemed normal to me, but after, I started questioning a lot of things. I write this letter therefore with a single question in my mind: What do you do when the ethical choice is impossible?

“... ethics is not a luxury...” you said as you took a deep breath, “It’s not something we teach after we’ve covered the ‘real’ engineering - the calculus, the mechanics, the design. Ethics is engineering. Because every choice we make, every tolerance we set, every material we specify, every simulation we run - these have consequences for real people living real lives.”

From this perspective, I understand that our ethical choices have real consequences on real people. But then, I’ve found myself wondering about the usually unnoticed ethical choices that engineers have to make daily, and that even now, I have to make as an Engineering student.

For instance, is it ethical for me to use pirated software as an engineering student?  
This year in school, I have to learn SolidAxis for a course. SolidAxis is the most popular, industry standard CAD Modelling software used in both engineering education and practice, and I guess the curriculum requires that we learn it.

As I was trying to understand more about the course, I learned that we all have to pirate the software for use in class. You may wonder, does my school not have licenses? And that was my first question too. It turns out, they do! They have three SolidAxis 2007 licenses.

Now, if we choose to ignore the fact that the topics in the curriculum are well beyond the scope of the SolidAxis 2007 feature set, we could do basic math to find that a class of 86 students using three software licenses would mean that approximately 29 students have to pair on each computer during classes. This just won’t happen. So, I need to find alternatives.

One alternative is to buy the software, or to use the most appropriate term, pay for a year’s subscription. Presently, SolidAxis student versions only apply for certain institutions, and when I checked, my school wasn’t listed. The professional license is $4,200/year. That’s roughly what my parents would earn in nine years combined (if they didn’t spend a single kobo). So that’s not an option I can take either.

This is why I have wrestled with the initial question I asked earlier. In my case, it seems the ethical thing to do is impossible, therefore I have taken the only option open to me.

As I write this email, I have the pirated version of the SolidAxis 2018 running on my PC. Now, when I think about your earlier speech again, it makes me wonder if unethical practices like this are part of the “real engineering” I am taught. I learned that everyone pirates this software, the professors here use pirated copies, my seniors use pirated copies, I even heard that professionals working on life changing projects in our community also use these same pirated copies.

I’ve been learning SolidAxis now for a few months, and I think I’m getting much better as the days go by. But at what cost? Do these kinds of compromises stop here, or is this only the beginning? Is this who I have to become to be an engineer? Is this what the system requires?

Dr Wilkerson, what can I do when ethical practice becomes impossible?

Warm regards,  
**Fola Adeoluwa**  
Mechatronics Engineering Undergraduate Student  
Microris University of Technology (MUT), Lagos
