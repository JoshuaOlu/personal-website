# Getting found: what the site does, and what only you can do

## Already built into the site
- Titles, descriptions, canonical links and link previews on every page.
- Structured data (JSON-LD) describing you as a person, your research, publications and writing, tied together.
- `sitemap.xml`, an Atom feed, `llms.txt`, and a clear About page with bios people can copy.
- AI policy: search and assistant crawlers allowed; training crawlers asked to stay out (`robots.txt`,
  `tdmrep.json`, `noai` meta tags). These are requests that well-behaved bots honour. They are not enforcement.

## What only you can do (this is where most of the ranking comes from)
1. Google Search Console: verify `joshua.olunlade.com`, submit `sitemap.xml`. Do the same in Bing Webmaster Tools.
2. ORCID: create or complete the profile, link the site, list every output.
3. Google Scholar profile, with the site as your homepage.
4. UCT CREE staff/student page and Care for Knowledge website: ask them to link to this site using your full name.
5. LinkedIn, GitHub, ResearchGate, Academia: same name, same one-line bio, link to the site. Add the links in `identity.yml` (`same_as_extra`).
6. Publish writing regularly on your topics (Engineering education in Nigeria and Africa, SIWES, ...). Use the words people search for.
7. Deposit papers in open repositories (UCT OpenUCT, Zenodo) and cite the site.
8. Speak, co-author and get cited. Links from other credible sites matter most.

Ranking takes weeks to months. Nothing here can guarantee a position.

## Writing that gets found
- One idea per piece, with the phrase people would search for in the title and the first paragraph.
- Fill in the summary and description (the editor shows how it will look on Google and LinkedIn).
- Add a caption and description to every picture. Use headings, so the contents box and search engines can see the structure.
- Put the newsletter signup and the feed to work: each piece is in `feed.xml`, the sitemap and the structured data automatically.
- Link to your own earlier pieces and papers, and ask others to link to you.

## Preview pictures
Writing without its own image gets an automatic on-brand card (title, topic, photo, name). See GUIDE.md. Keep titles under about 70 characters.
